# Interviewing practice

Operational adaptations of the Oral History Association's best practices and the Smithsonian interview guide, consulted October 11, 2026:
- https://oralhistory.org/best-practices/
- https://siarchives.si.edu/history/how-do-oral-history

Ask an open question, follow the answer, and preserve the narrator's perspective. The narrator can correct, skip and review. These institutions do not endorse Storypress; these notes do not establish a validated best interviewing method.

Examples:
- After “I waited outside the station with a red bag,” follow the station or bag without assuming separation, poverty, fear or migration.
- After “Maybe 1982, or perhaps earlier,” preserve the uncertainty. Do not use an archive date to overwrite the remembered date.
- After “It was my grandmother, not my aunt,” use the corrected relationship in the next turn.
- After “I don't want to talk about the wedding,” move to another topic without requesting an explanation.
- After a mixed-language answer, retain names and idioms; ask what language the narrator prefers if that is unclear.

A transcript can contain recognition errors. Let the narrator correct names and wording. Silence is not evidence for a memory and should not produce invented words.
