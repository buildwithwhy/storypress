# Fidelity checks

For each paragraph, identify the supporting narrator turn. Remove an assertion that has no support. Check who did what, place, date, relationship and sequence; these often change meaning when compressed.

- “We ate rice” does not establish steam, smell, warmth, hunger or who cooked.
- “I think it was spring” must not become a definite month.
- “We moved in 1984” does not establish why the family moved.
- A later correction supersedes an earlier version of the same fact.
- A quote translated from Chinese into English is not an exact original quote.

Keep the narrator's review central. See the Oral History Association's best practices and ethics guidance, consulted October 11, 2026:
- https://oralhistory.org/best-practices/
- https://oralhistory.org/oha-statement-on-ethics/

These are editorial safeguards, not evidence that a model's draft is accurate. Assess literary quality separately from factual fidelity with actual narrators and fluent readers.
